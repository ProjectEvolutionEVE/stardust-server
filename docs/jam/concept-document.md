# Stardust - Terra Deleta: Game Concept Document (DRAFT for Eric)

*Zoud GameLab Financial Literacy Jam · theme: Make FinLit Fun*

## 1. Game Overview

**Stardust - Terra Deleta** is a real-time space life sim and economy game. The inner planets of our solar system are
gone; their remains circle the Sun as four rings of debris. Players live aboard the research starship WSC Alexander von
Humboldt, parked beside a mysterious wormhole beyond Jupiter, and build a life and a career from what they find, learn,
make and trade.

**The story, in short.** A wormhole opened near the far side of Jupiter. The research starship WSC Alexander von
Humboldt went to study it and found, on the other side, a twin of our universe: a New Earth with air, water, forests
and animals, but no humans and no sign that any had ever lived there. After a 12-month survey proved it could sustain
human life, the ship came home through the wormhole to find our solar system devastated. Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars
and most of their moons have been destroyed by something no one understands, and an unknown civilisation of scavengers,
silent and unarmed, is sifting through the debris. The crew rebuild, study, and look for answers, and the New Earth
waits on the other side.

**Inara.** A real person (her story is told in the novel): an officer in the Starship's command centre, the Tower, and the one voice that talks the player through the whole game: launch
clearances, finds, warnings, and the story as it unfolds. She likes fireflies, has a secret love of chocolate, and keeps
an old shirt that still smells of cedar, for someone she hopes to find again. Players hear her in 11 languages (or read
her words on screen in any language).

**The full story** is a novel (title ▢, Eric), offered in the game in text and as an audiobook. A short
illustrated, voiced slideshow tells the backstory before a new player's first launch. An Arabic edition of the novel
and slideshow is planned, as a thank-you to the UAE, the jam's host country.

- **Genre:** space life sim, mining and economy, with careers you learn through a shipboard library.
- **Platform:** mobile first (Godot 4; iOS and Android), then PC (Steam) and the web.
- **Audience:** a general audience of players who like exploration, crafting and economies; also anyone who wants to
  get better with money without being lectured.
- **What's different:** money is not a minigame here. It is the life of the ship. Every career, every trip and every
  meal runs on choices about earning, saving, spending and borrowing, with real consequences that unfold in real time.

## 2. Core Gameplay

**The loop:** wake up in your quarters → study in the Starship Library to earn the skills you need → fly out to a debris
ring → find a field with your scanner (a hiss, a rising tone, two bearings to lock it) → collect real-sized pieces of ore
that must fit your hold → warp home and unload them with a receipt → sell on a live market, or save, invest and plan →
rest, because your body has limits.

- **Objectives:** build a career and a life: rise through a career's five bars (Pilot to Captain, Trader to CEO, and
  more), afford a better ship or a production plant, reach long-term goals.
- **Challenges and decisions:** which ring and field to work; what fits your hold; sell now or wait for a better price;
  save, lock credits in a term deposit, or borrow; spend on food that helps you work or on treats that make you crash.
- **Progression:** library books and five-question tests earn career bars (gold for a perfect score, silver otherwise);
  each bar takes more study than the last, and gives a smaller, still valuable, skill increase.
- **Why it's fun:** a true-scale solar system, a scanner you play by ear, a Starship you dock with by autopilot, rare
  finds (one diamond in 100,000 is near-flawless), and Inara, the officer in the Tower who talks you through it in 11 languages.

## 3. Financial Literacy Integration

Financial concepts are the game's systems, not quizzes bolted on:

| Concept | How it plays |
|---|---|
| Earning and income | Every trip's ore is real income, sold on a live order book whose prices rise and fall. |
| Budgeting | Income against costs: time, rest, food, printing fees for blueprints, loan payments. |
| Saving and goals | Savings goals at the Starship Bank ("a new ship: 2,010 of 5,000") with progress bars. |
| Interest earned | A savings account and 2, 4 and 8-week term deposits; closing a term early gives up the interest. |
| Borrowing and debt | The Bank weighs each loan (standing, income, debt, purpose) and shows the full cost before you sign. |
| Good debt vs bad debt | A loan for a bigger hold pays for itself; a loan for snacks is gone tomorrow, and costs more. |
| Paying off early | The payoff amount includes interest only to today, so paying early visibly saves credits. |
| Credit standing | On-time payments raise it (cheaper, bigger loans); late payments add fees and lower it. |
| Records | Every transfer gives a receipt with a code anyone can verify. |
| Risk and patience | Market prices move; selling everything at the bottom costs you. |
| Delayed gratification | Food and drinks: a sugary burst now, a crash later; good food helps you work longer. |

Every career's library books also teach money in that field (a miner's uneven income, costing a build, budgeting a
research project), so whichever path a player chooses, they learn to manage money in it.

**What players should take away:** money decisions compound over time; borrowing is a tool that must earn more than it
costs; saving toward a named goal works; paying debt early saves money; short-term treats have long-term costs.

## 4. Visual Style & Player Experience

- **Look:** realistic and clean: a true-scale solar system with NASA imagery of the Sun, Jupiter and Saturn, NASA's
  asteroid models for the debris, and a sleek research starship. Instruments are transparent, thin red outlines over the
  view, like a pilot's heads-up display.
- **Tone:** calm, curious and hopeful: rebuilding after loss, learning, and making a life far from home.
- **Interface and accessibility:** big touch controls for one thumb on a phone; sound cues backed by visual ones (the
  scanner bar, the gold rim cue, on-screen messages alongside what Inara says (full subtitles are planned)); Inara's voice in 11 languages, or none.
- **Inclusive by design:** players choose a name from the game's list and a rank they earn, no real names; no player
  chat (a safe, quiet universe); suitable for a general audience.

## 5. Development & Future Plans

**In the current build:** sign-in; Private Quarters (bed, desk, closet, bookshelf, filing cabinet); the Starship Library
with books and tests; the 3D Starship and docking bay; the true-scale solar system, warp and radar; debris fields,
scanner, collecting and unloading; the live market and blueprint auctions; Inara's recorded voice; resting in bed or in
the cockpit; the Starship Bank with savings, term deposits, loans and savings goals.

**Next:** career ladders wired to the library (books for every bar are being written), uniforms, food and drink with
their effects, massive shared debris fields, player-designed ships and equipment through the blueprint system, and the
phone release.

**Risks and how we handle them:** content volume (hundreds of library books: written in batches and fact-checked);
balance of the economy (every number is a live setting tuned in playtesting); accuracy of money lessons (checked
against established financial-education sources).
