Zoud GameLab Financial Literacy (FinLit) Jam
Game Concept & GDD Document Starter Kit

A Game Concept Document or Game Design Document (GDD) helps you explain your game's vision, how it works and what makes it special.
For Zoud GameLab, your document is particularly useful because it helps judges understand your game's financial literacy objectives, creative vision and potential for further development beyond the jam.
Use the following six sections as a simple starting template.

1. Game Overview
Introduce your game and its central idea.
* Game title and genre
* Brief description of the concept
* Intended platform (PC, mobile, browser, etc.)
* Target audience
* What makes your game interesting or different?
Tip: Start with trying to describe your game in just two or three sentences.

2. Core Gameplay
Explain what players actually do.
* What is the player's main objective?
* What actions can they perform?
* What challenges or decisions will they face?
* How does the game progress?
* What makes the experience fun and engaging?
Tip: Focus on your core gameplay loop—the actions players repeat throughout the game.

________________
3. Financial Literacy Integration
This is especially important for Zoud GameLab!
Explain how your game explores financial literacy through gameplay.
* Which financial concepts does your game address?
* How are those concepts connected to gameplay mechanics?
* What choices or consequences help players explore these ideas?
* What do you hope players will understand after playing?
Example: A resource-management game could introduce budgeting by challenging players to balance limited income against competing expenses and unexpected events.

4. Visual Style & Player Experience
Give readers an idea of what your game looks and feels like.
* Art style and visual direction
* Setting, world or narrative (if relevant)
* General tone and atmosphere
* Important interface or accessibility considerations
Tip: Screenshots, sketches, diagrams or mood boards can help communicate your ideas quickly.

5. Development & Future Plans
Explain what you've achieved and where the project could go next.
* What features are included in your current prototype?
* What would you like to develop or improve?
* What are your main development priorities?
* What challenges or risks might you need to overcome?
Tip: Be realistic! A focused, achievable plan is more useful than a long list of ambitious features.

6. Your Team
Briefly introduce the people behind your game.
* Team members and their roles
* Relevant experience or skills
* Any additional expertise or support that might be needed
For solo developers, simply describe your own role and the skills you bring to the project.

A Few Final Tips
* Keep it clear and concise. Avoid unnecessary technical language or lengthy explanations.
* Show, don't just tell. Use diagrams, screenshots or examples where they help explain your ideas.
* Focus on the player. Explain what people actually do and experience in your game.
* Connect the mechanics to financial literacy. Make it clear how your game explores the jam theme.
* Be realistic. Distinguish between what you've already created and what you hope to develop in the future.
* Make it easy to read. Use clear headings, short paragraphs and a logical structure.

Ready to Submit?
If you're entering your game for Zoud GameLab awards or development funding, remember to include your Game Concept Document or GDD as a PDF, shared through a publicly accessible link that doesn't require login or permission requests.
Important Note: Your project budget is a separate submission document.
