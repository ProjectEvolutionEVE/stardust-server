### Where the novel differs from the game's Story Bible and rules

1. **"WSC".** The novel says World Space *Command*. The game (STORY-005) says World Space *Consortium*. Eric should pick one before any novel text is reused.
2. **Cigars versus chocolate.** The Bible (2026-09-30) replaced the cigar with a secret love of chocolate. The novel has cigars in Ch12, 16, 19 and 27 and **no chocolate anywhere**. The two do not conflict in the game, but nothing in the novel supports the chocolate, so it stays game-only. Do not quote the cigar scenes.
3. **Whose shirt.** The Bible's open question 6 ("whose was the cedar shirt?") is answered in the novel: Andrew Mago's, via his grandfather. That is the biggest late reveal available.
4. **Fireflies.** The Bible only says she likes them. In the novel they come from her mother and from Andrew's meadow.
5. **The Tower.** The Bible's Tower is a command and control centre, and CLAUDE.md rule 9 makes it the bridge. The novel agrees: she works on the bridge, and "Control Tower" is a crew nickname for her station and for her (Ch21). The novel does not call the place "the Tower". It also never gives her a rank. Do not invent one.
6. **The scavengers and debris fields** do not exist in the Master. The novel ends on the shock of the empty inner system, with no looters. Anything about scavengers is game-only.
7. **The cause.** The Bible and rule 7 say nobody knows what destroyed the planets. The novel quietly says more (Ch33, Ch35): seven faint objects arrived from the direction of Orion, one a month, and Mars, Earth, Venus, Mercury and the Moon went in turn. This is the novel's mystery. The game's Inara must not state it (see §4).
8. **The crew size.** The novel has 114 crew. Eleven leave on the shuttle (ten once Hayashi stays, per Rewrite-Notes). The game's "ninety to a hundred and twenty" is compatible.
9. **Dover.** The crew's first landing site on the new Earth is Dover's cliffs, where an oak tree stands where her house would be (Ch28; Ch30). That fits the game's "place each recovered piece at its own coordinates" and is a free hook.

## 2. The arc: what a reader learns, and when

1. **Chs 1-2:** a watcher with a gift, a loving family, a telescope, Jupiter and a longing to go "up". The reader meets mother, father, the pendant and the glow-worms.
2. **Chs 3-8:** she gives up Indiana for her mother. The strong day and her mother's death. The promise: "go and see the fireflies".
3. **Chs 5-6, 9-13:** cryptography, the convent letter, the Listening Post, Andrew, the decoder, and their relationship begins (Ch13, "The Grin").
4. **Ch14:** fireflies and cedar; the shirt; the happiest years.
5. **Chs 15-17:** the signal, found by her alone at 2 a.m. "Somebody's talking."
6. **Chs 18-20:** the ship, Andrew's freezer fear, the promise "Never again", the parting.
7. **Chs 21-22:** the Humboldt; she becomes "Control Tower".
8. **Chs 23-27:** the wormhole, the lost probes, the Petrel crossing and the empty twin Earth. The "last calls" to her father and Andrew, and the crossing.
9. **Chs 28-34:** a year on the new Earth and the letters that can't be sent. The white flower ("Now we match") connects her to her mother.
10. **Ch35:** the return; the inner system is gone; the unanswered "Humboldt to anyone"; letter No. 359.
11. **Held back (Rewrite-Notes):** the signal she hears is slightly different. The truth that Earth survives in a third copy is reserved for the **sequel**.

**Suggested game order (same order as the book, from the least to the most personal):** craft and character first (the watcher, the telescope, the gulls), then her mother and the fireflies, then the shirt and the promise, then the unanswered call.

## 4. What NOT to reveal early, and what to keep out of a general-audience game

**Hold until late (or never):**
- **Andrew Mago**, the promise "Never again" and the cedar box with "Never" on it. These are the emotional payload that makes the shirt matter. Lines 16 and 17 should arrive late, and only if Eric answers Bible Q6.
- **The ending, Ch35.** Do not hint that her father and Andrew were left on a planet that is now gone, and do not mention the seven objects from Orion.
- **Reyes's discovery of the cause** (the "string of beads"). The game's Tower must never state a cause or even narrow it down.
- **The Rewrite-Notes twist** (a third copy of our universe; nobody they love died) and Inara's changed-signal discovery. These are held for the sequel. The game must not leak them. They also fit the Bible's "never say never".
- **Her surname Worthington.** Only the first name appears in the game so far. Eric should decide.
- **Her mother's illness and death** as plain fact. Line 11 only gestures at it.

**Content to keep out of a general-audience game (rule 5):**
- **Suicide and self-harm.** Rewrite-Notes plans a volunteer being talked down from ending his life, and Hayashi's attempted detonation at the engine core with the Captain shooting his friend. Not in the Master yet, and **not for game lines**. The Notes suggest a crisis-line note at the back of the book (988 US; Samaritans 116 123 UK); the game needs the same care if it ever touches grief.
- **Cigars, bourbon and smoking.** The novel has scenes on the levee, the observation deck and the engine room. The Bible already retired the cigar, so use chocolate or tea only.
- **Child illness, bullying and grief** (Chs 1, 4, 7, 8). Keep references gentle and never graphic.
- **Real people.** Alexander von Humboldt is a real historical figure. The game's STORY lines already cover him, so Inara needs nothing new. Do not use any real names of living people.
- **Realism and violence.** Chs 29-32 contain an animal injury (a geologist hurt by a mammoth) and a storm rescue. Not for the early game.
- **Open consistency flags** (Character-Bible §2): the novel contradicts itself on the glow-worm scene (car or walking: Ch7 vs Ch28) and on the letter numbering. Do not quote Ch28 or the letter numbers as exact facts until Eric fixes them.
