# Inara's novel: outline (draft for Eric's approval)

**Working title: _The Cedar Drawer_ (WORKING TITLE ONLY, not chosen).** Draft v0.1 · 2026-10-20.

Inara is the protagonist and first-person narrator. She tells her life before her career aboard the Starship, from
her quarters aboard the WSC Alexander von Humboldt after the ship's return through the wormhole. The frame never
reveals what destroyed the four planets (nobody knows), never says everyone on Earth died ("did anyone on the planet
survive?" stays open), and never names a player.

**Canon used (Story Bible, `docs/STORY-BIBLE.md`, and approved Tower lines):** the ship is a large exploratory science
and research starship; Inara works in its command and control centre, the Tower, guiding smaller vessels' departures
and returns; she loves fireflies; she has a secret love of chocolate; she keeps an old tattered shirt that smells of
cedar, from someone she once knew and hopes to find again against long odds; the WSC is a civilian space agency; she
started at the bottom (INARA-STORY-002); the wormhole opened near the far side of Jupiter; a volunteer team crossed
first; the 12-month survey of the New Earth; the return to a devastated system (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars and most
of their moons); the scavengers; the crew are Earth's people too.

## The arc
A woman who learned early to watch small lights in the dark becomes the voice that brings ships home. Each part of
her life teaches one piece of the Tower: patience, attention, letting go, and holding on anyway. The question under
the whole book is the drawer: whether some things are kept because they are lost, or because they may yet be found.

## Chapters

**Part One: The Lights (childhood)**
1. **The Drawer.** Frame: her quarters after the return. She opens the drawer, then goes back to the summer she first
   caught fireflies in a jar and let them go. Her grandmother's hidden chocolate tin. The first lesson: you can watch a
   light without owning it. (Written: `chapter-01.md`.)
2. **Counting.** A child who counts things: blinks, stars, the seconds between lightning and thunder. A school she is
   not good at, until someone shows her that counting is a kind of listening.
3. **The Woodshop.** She meets the person the shirt belongs to: a young woodworker who smells of cedar shavings. Never
   named in the book; Inara calls them "you" when she speaks to them.
4. **Night Sky.** The two of them on a roof watching satellites cross. The first time she wants to go up.

**Part Two: The Bottom Rung (training)**
5. **Application.** She applies to the WSC and is turned down once. Tries again.
6. **Trainee.** She starts at the bottom (STORY-002: "I did too, once"): cleaning filters, logging cargo, learning the
   radio by listening to other people use it well.
7. **The Shirt.** A goodbye. The shirt left behind on a chair, or given; she never decides which story is true.
8. **Small Voices.** Her first time on a traffic desk. She learns that a calm voice is a tool.

**Part Three: The Yard (the ship, before)**
9. **The Ship in the Yard.** She sees the Humboldt being finished in orbit and asks to serve aboard her.
10. **Selection.** Tests, rejections, a long wait. Packing: the drawer goes with her, and a hidden supply of chocolate.
11. **The Tower.** Her first shifts in command and control, guiding the smaller vessels out and home.
12. **Jupiter.** The wormhole opens near the far side of Jupiter. The sensor arrays. The whole crew holding its breath.

**Part Four: The Far Side**
13. **Volunteers.** The small volunteer team crosses first; she talks them out and waits to talk them home.
14. **Twin.** The whole ship crosses. A world with air, water, forests and animals, and no people, ever.
15. **Fireflies Again.** On the New Earth, at dusk, she sees them. She does not catch one.
16. **Twelve Months.** The survey confirms the world can sustain people. The crew's joy. She writes a letter home she
    means to send on return.

**Part Five: Home**
17. **The Crossing Back.** The return through the wormhole. The devastated system. What the crew sees, and what no one
    can explain; every theory, none of them proven.
18. **The Looters.** The scavengers in the debris fields: no weapons, no answer to the hails.
19. **The Question.** "Did anyone on the planet survive?" The ship is full of Earth's people, still alive, and nobody
    rules the rest out. Inara takes her seat in the Tower again and starts bringing the small ships home.
20. **The Cedar Drawer.** Back to the frame. She keeps the drawer closed most nights, and open on some. The odds are
    low. Never say never.

## Inventions to approve (not in the Story Bible; kept small)
1. **Her grandmother**, who raised her for some summers and kept a hidden tin of chocolate (the origin of her secret
   love of chocolate).
2. **Her childhood place:** a small, unnamed town at the edge of a cedar wood with a creek, where fireflies came out in
   early summer. No country is named.
3. **The shirt's owner is a woodworker**, which is why the shirt smells of cedar. Never named, gender never stated;
   Inara addresses them as "you".
4. **A first rejection** from the WSC before she was accepted (Ch. 5).
5. **Her first job on a traffic desk** before she served aboard the Humboldt (Ch. 8), and **seeing the ship in the
   orbital yard** (Ch. 9; the yard itself is in the approved Tower lines STORY-021/022).
6. **She was aboard for the whole mission**, including the volunteer crossing and the 12 months (canon puts the ship
   there; whether Inara was aboard from the start is not stated).
7. **Fireflies on the New Earth** (Ch. 15). Canon lists insects there; fireflies specifically are an invention.
8. **She hums** (a small habit in Ch. 1), with no link to the scavengers' love of music unless Eric wants one.
